01 · Overview

What it is

HumanPost sells organic distribution as a service. A brand that would normally run one account and post a few times a week instead gets 10, 100 or 1,000 dedicated accounts. Each one is run by a different person in the US, and each one posts the brand's videos or photo carousels.

The brand never talks to the posters. It works through a dashboard, or lets its own AI agent drive everything through an API or an MCP server. The site describes the job as owning the posting workflow end to end, from content instructions to proof and reporting.

“Introducing HumanPost — hire an army of Americans to post your TikToks. Get 10, 100, or 1,000 dedicated social accounts for your company.”

  • Run by real people in the US
  • Warmed up specifically for your niche
  • Dedicated accounts just for your brand
  • Zero VPNs
Mehak Vohra, launch post on X, 28 August 2026

Volume wins on short-form

TikTok and Reels show posts to an interest-based feed, so new accounts can reach strangers. Many accounts testing many hooks find winners faster than one account can.

Real people, not bots

The pitch rests on verified US posters, real accounts and no VPNs. HumanPost presents this as the difference between it and automated account farms. Company claim

Creators are slow to manage

Briefing, contracting, chasing and paying dozens of creators is the work HumanPost takes over. Its posters execute instructions and don't negotiate.

02 · Workflow

How a campaign runs

Every account goes through the same lifecycle. The FAQ says a campaign can be set up in minutes: upload content, add instructions, and HumanPost routes the tasks to people.

  1. Design the accounts

    You choose each account's profile photo, username, bio and terms, for TikTok or Instagram.

  2. A poster is assigned

    Each account gets a US-based poster automatically. The poster creates the account by hand to your spec.

  3. Warm-up

    You share niche keywords and product context. The accounts engage with related content before they post anything, so each one starts out looking like it belongs in your niche.

  4. Send content

    You upload videos or photos; carousels are supported. Posters publish only the content you approve and have no creative control.

  5. Comment tasks

    Each plan includes a quota of comment tasks, also completed by hand.

  6. Proof and reporting

    HumanPost collects proof for every completed task. Posts, views, comments, likes and experiments show up in one dashboard.

  7. Boost the winners

    Plans include “SparkCode requests”. On TikTok, a Spark Ads code lets an advertiser put paid spend behind an organic post that is already working. Our reading; the site doesn't define it

04 · Developers

Built for AI agents

The homepage pitches a second kind of customer: AI agents. It invites you to let your agent task US-based humans with creating warmed-up social accounts and posting content, through the HumanPost API or MCP server. A CLI is mentioned as well.

The logic is simple. Agents can now produce video and carousel content in bulk, but they can't hold a phone in Ohio. HumanPost sells itself as the last mile between generated content and a real feed.

From API key to queued post in three calls

Create a key under Dashboard → Settings → API & MCP and send it as a Bearer token. The docs say to keep keys server-side and to send a unique Idempotency-Key with every post so retries are safe.

# 1. Find the account IDs you want to publish to
GET  /v2/accounts
Authorization: Bearer $HUMANPOST_API_KEY

# 2. Hand over media by URL
POST /v2/uploads
{ "kind": "video", "sourceUrl": "https://cdn.example.com/hook-07.mp4" }

# 3. Queue one post across several accounts
POST /v2/posts
Idempotency-Key: 3f9a1c2e-…
{ "mediaIds": ["med_…"],
  "accountIds": ["acc_…", "acc_…"],
  "caption": "day 3 of using this app to plan trips" }

Reconstructed from the public quickstart. Paths and parameters follow its description; exact field names may differ. The examples call a publishingApi on a Google Cloud Functions host, which points to a lean serverless stack.

09 · Scrutiny

Reception and risks

  • Platform rules

    TikTok's Community Guidelines have sections on Deceptive Behaviors and Fake Engagement and on Commercial Disclosure and Paid Marketing, and TikTok can remove accounts tied to coordinated inauthentic behavior. Brand-designed persona accounts run by paid posters sit close to those lines. Indexed copies of HumanPost's site don't address ad disclosure.

  • Account ownership

    The public pages don't say who owns the accounts, who holds the logins, or what happens to the accounts and their followers when you cancel.

  • Pricing backlash

    Replies to the launch thread attacked the price; one called the $250 per-post view cap “crazy”. Vohra says customers answered in the replies and explained why it's worth paying for.

  • Unaudited numbers

    The network size, the US-only and no-VPN claims, and every CPM figure come from the company. No third-party audit was found.

  • A very young company

    Founded in 2026 and still hiring its first strategists. Funding is not public. An aggregator lists one employee, which is probably out of date.

11 · For your idea

Building your own

What HumanPost gets right, and where a similar product could go. The suggestions favor versions that stay inside platform rules, because those are the ones that last and that larger brands will buy.

Six lessons from the teardown

Sell the finished task

Buyers don't pay for access to posters. They pay for posts that happened, with proof. The routing, reminders and proof collection are the actual product.

Two-speed pricing

A cheap entry plan where cost follows views, capped per post, then a flat plan once you know what works. The deposit covers view charges that arrive after the post.

Make agents a customer on day one

API, MCP server and llms.txt from the start. Content-generating agents need somewhere to send their output, and they don't care about your UI.

Constrain the worker

Posters get zero creative control. That makes quality predictable, keeps tasks small, and lets you scale supply without training people.

Supply is the moat

A live counter of available people, location checks and niche-matched warm-up are hard to copy. Software is the easy half.

Launch loud, let customers defend you

A provocative founder thread got about 300K views. The price backlash became a stage for customers to explain the value.

Directions worth exploring

A disclosed creator network

The same task router, but posts go out from posters' real personal accounts with the platform's paid-partnership label. Slower to scale, but compliant, and sellable to brands whose legal teams would reject persona accounts.

Agent-to-human tasks beyond social

The orchestration layer works for product photos, in-store checks, usability tests, local flyering or translation QA. Stay away from reviews: the FTC's 2024 rule bans fake reviews and testimonials.

Markets HumanPost doesn't serve

HumanPost is US-only. Brands entering other regions need native speakers who know local trends and slang, from South Asia to the Gulf to Latin America.

The supply-side app

A task inbox for workers with proof capture, payout tracking and reputation. Every network like this needs one, and few build it well.

An experiment engine

Test hooks and formats across many disclosed accounts, then report which creative wins with real statistics, and feed the winners to ads through Spark-style codes.

Questions to answer before you build

  • Who owns each account, and who holds the logins?
  • How are workers recruited, verified, paid and taxed?
  • What do each platform's terms say about managed and multiple accounts?
  • How do you prove a task really happened?
  • How is sponsorship disclosed on every post?
  • What stops a worker from posting off-brand content on a client account?
  • What does a post cost you in worker pay, and what is your margin at $4.50 a post?
  • What happens to a campaign when a platform restricts an account?
12 · Disambiguation

Same name, different products

humanpost.com and humanpost.co serve the same product described above; the docs and the team's email live on .co. Several unrelated projects use the name too.

humanpost.app

A free Chrome extension for growing on X, with reply scoring, a tweet checker and an “Algo Analyzer”.

humanpost.io

A human-only social network concept. AI use is banned and there is no algorithmic ranking.

humanpost.site

Proves a LinkedIn post was written by a person by recording the writing process, then shows a “Human Score”.

Daniel Ost's HumanPost

A February 2026 side project that rewrites AI text to sound like a real person.
